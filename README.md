# Training & Development Portfolio

## Automotive Systems Fundamentals

A portfolio project demonstrating end-to-end instructional and learning program design, from defining measurable objectives to developing learning activities, educational content, assessments, and evaluation.

This portfolio demonstrates the process of transforming a subject area into a structured learning experience, from defining measurable learning objectives through content development, learning activities, practical application, assessment, and evaluation.

---

## Program Overview

**Program Title:** Automotive Systems Fundamentals

**Target Audience:** Beginner-level vocational and technical learners

**Delivery Format:** Blended learning — instructor-led sessions, demonstrations, discussions, and practical activities

**Suggested Duration:** 5 modules / approximately 15 learning sessions

### Program Goal

To provide learners with a foundational understanding of major automotive systems while developing basic practical inspection, problem-solving, and diagnostic skills.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the program, learners should be able to:

- Identify the purpose and major components of common automotive systems.
- Explain how major vehicle systems operate and interact.
- Apply appropriate workshop safety procedures.
- Use basic technical vocabulary related to automotive systems.
- Carry out basic inspection and fault-identification activities.
- Apply a structured approach to simple troubleshooting.
- Demonstrate learning through quizzes, practical tasks, assignments, and a final assessment.

## Instructional Design Approach

Each module follows a consistent learning pathway:

**Prior Knowledge → Instruction → Demonstration → Guided Practice → Independent Application → Assessment → Feedback**

The program combines:

- Clear learning objectives
- Structured educational content
- Demonstrations
- Practical activities
- Formative assessment
- Feedback
- Final evaluation

This approach allows learners to progress from understanding concepts to applying them in practical situations.

---

## Sample Module: Engine Systems

### Learning Outcomes

Learners should be able to:

1. Identify the spark plug, piston, cylinder, and crankshaft.
2. State the basic function of each component.
3. Explain at an introductory level how combustion produces mechanical movement.
4. Carry out a basic visual inspection safely.

### Learning Activities

- Examine a labeled engine diagram.
- Identify components on a real or model engine.
- Observe a basic inspection demonstration.
- Complete a guided component-identification exercise.
- Work through a simple engine fault scenario.

### Formative Assessment

Example questions:

1. What is the function of a piston?
2. What does a spark plug do in a petrol engine?
3. What is the role of the crankshaft?
4. Name two safety precautions to observe before inspecting an engine.

---

## Assessment Strategy

Assessment is integrated throughout the program rather than being limited to the final session.

### Assessment Methods

- Quizzes
- Practical tasks
- Assignments
- Case studies
- Observation checklists
- Final assessment
- Capstone activity

### Assessment Criteria

Learners are evaluated on:

- Technical understanding
- Practical execution
- Problem-solving ability
- Communication
- Safety awareness

---

## Capstone Assessment

Learners receive a simple vehicle fault scenario.

They are required to:

1. Identify the relevant automotive system.
2. Identify possible causes.
3. Propose appropriate inspection steps.
4. Demonstrate safe working procedures.
5. Explain their recommended next action.

The facilitator evaluates the learner using a structured assessment rubric.

---

## Completion Criteria

To successfully complete the program, learners must:

- Complete the required learning sessions.
- Meet the minimum score for the final knowledge assessment.
- Complete required practical activities safely.
- Demonstrate understanding of core automotive systems.
- Demonstrate a basic structured approach to diagnosis and maintenance.

---

## Program Design Rationale

The program progresses from foundational knowledge to guided practice and independent application.

Learning objectives are aligned with learning activities and assessment methods. Practical exercises provide opportunities for learners to apply concepts, while formative assessments provide feedback throughout the learning journey.

The structure also supports different learning preferences through explanation, visual materials, demonstrations, discussion, hands-on practice, and assessment.
