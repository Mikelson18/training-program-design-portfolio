# Training & Development Portfolio

## Automotive Systems Fundamentals

> **Portfolio project demonstrating an end-to-end approach to instructional and learning program design.**

This project demonstrates the process of transforming a technical subject area into a structured learning experience — from defining measurable objectives and organizing content to designing learning activities, assessments, practical application, and evaluation.

---

## 1. About the Designer

I have a background in **Industrial Technology Education**, with practical exposure to automotive systems and technical education.

My experience developing lesson plans, learning objectives, instructional content, practical activities, and assessments has shaped my approach to designing structured learning experiences.

This portfolio applies that foundation to a complete sample learning program and demonstrates how subject-matter knowledge can be translated into a clear, learner-focused training experience.

---

## 2. Program Overview

### Program Title
**Automotive Systems Fundamentals**

### Target Audience
Beginner-level vocational and technical learners.

### Delivery Format
Blended learning combining:

- Instructor-led lessons
- Visual explanations and demonstrations
- Group discussions
- Guided practical activities
- Knowledge checks and assessments

### Suggested Duration
**5 modules / approximately 15 learning sessions**

### Program Goal

To provide learners with a foundational understanding of major automotive systems while developing basic practical inspection, problem-solving, safety, and diagnostic skills.

---

## 3. Program Learning Objectives

By the end of the program, learners should be able to:

1. Identify the purpose and major components of common automotive systems.
2. Explain, in simple technical terms, how major vehicle systems operate and interact.
3. Apply appropriate workshop safety procedures and basic technical vocabulary.
4. Carry out basic inspection and fault-identification activities using a structured process.
5. Demonstrate learning through quizzes, practical tasks, assignments, and a final assessment.

### Objective Design Approach

The objectives are written to describe observable learner outcomes. They move from basic knowledge and identification toward application, problem-solving, and demonstration of competency.

## 5. Instructional Design Approach

Each module follows a consistent learning pathway:

**Prior Knowledge → Instruction → Demonstration → Guided Practice → Independent Application → Assessment → Feedback → Recap**

### Step 1: Activate Prior Knowledge
Learners are introduced to questions or familiar examples that connect the new topic to what they already know.

### Step 2: Introduce the Concept
The instructor presents the core concepts using clear explanations, diagrams, examples, and technical vocabulary appropriate for beginner learners.

### Step 3: Demonstrate
The instructor demonstrates a component, process, inspection procedure, or worked example.

### Step 4: Guided Practice
Learners perform an activity with support and feedback from the instructor.

### Step 5: Independent Application
Learners apply what they have learned to a practical task, problem, or scenario.

### Step 6: Assess
Knowledge and practical performance are checked through quizzes, worksheets, observation, and practical tasks.

### Step 7: Feedback and Recap
Learners receive feedback on their performance and review the key points before progressing to the next topic.

## 6. Sample Module Build — Engine Systems

This section demonstrates how a single module is developed from objectives through activities and assessment.

### Module Focus

**Understanding major engine components and their basic functions.**

### Learning Outcomes

By the end of the module, learners should be able to:

- Identify the spark plug, piston, cylinder, and crankshaft.
- State the basic function of each component.
- Describe at an introductory level how combustion produces mechanical movement.
- Use a simple checklist to inspect selected engine components safely.

### Learning Activities

1. Examine a labeled engine diagram and identify familiar components.
2. Use a real or model engine for component demonstration where available.
3. Observe a basic visual inspection demonstration.
4. Complete a guided component-identification exercise.
5. Work through a simple engine fault scenario.

### Educational Content

**Spark Plug:** Produces the spark that ignites the air-fuel mixture in a petrol engine.

**Piston:** Moves inside the cylinder and transfers force from combustion to the connecting rod.

**Cylinder:** Provides the space in which the piston moves and combustion occurs.

**Crankshaft:** Converts the piston's reciprocating movement into rotational movement.

### Formative Assessment

1. What is the function of a piston?
2. What does a spark plug do in a petrol engine?
3. What is the role of the crankshaft?
4. Name two safety precautions to observe before inspecting an engine.

### Practical Activity

Learners are given an engine diagram or available training component and asked to:

- Locate selected components.
- Identify each component.
- State its basic function.
- Follow the provided safety checklist.

## 7. Assessment Strategy

Assessment is embedded throughout the learning journey rather than being limited to the final stage.

### Formative Assessment

Used during learning to check understanding and identify areas requiring clarification.

Examples:

- Short quizzes
- Oral questioning
- Component identification
- Worksheets
- Guided troubleshooting
- Instructor observation

### Summative Assessment

Used at the end of the program to determine whether learners can demonstrate the intended knowledge and practical skills.

Examples:

- Final knowledge assessment
- Practical task
- Troubleshooting scenario
- Capstone activity

---

## 8. Sample Assessment Rubric

| Criterion | Excellent | Competent | Needs Improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Technical Understanding** | Accurately explains concepts and relationships | Explains main concepts with minor gaps | Shows major gaps or confusion |
| **Practical Execution** | Completes task safely and independently | Completes task with limited guidance | Requires substantial guidance |
| **Problem Solving** | Uses a logical diagnostic process | Follows a basic troubleshooting sequence | Approach is inconsistent |
| **Communication** | Clear, structured, and technically appropriate | Generally clear | Unclear or incomplete |

The rubric provides a consistent framework for evaluating both knowledge and practical performance.

---

## 9. Capstone / Final Assessment

### Scenario

Learners receive a simple vehicle fault scenario.

They are required to:

1. Identify the vehicle system involved.
2. State possible causes of the fault.
3. Propose appropriate inspection steps.
4. Identify relevant safety precautions.
5. Communicate the recommended next action.

### Assessment Focus

The facilitator evaluates:

- Technical understanding
- Safety awareness
- Diagnostic reasoning
- Practical process
- Communication

The structured rubric is used to support consistent evaluation.

---

## 10. Completion Criteria

A learner successfully completes the program when they:

- Complete the required learning sessions.
- Meet the minimum score for the final knowledge assessment.
- Complete required practical activities safely.
- Demonstrate understanding of core automotive systems.
- Demonstrate a basic structured approach to diagnosis and maintenance.

---

## 11. Learning Evaluation

Program effectiveness can be evaluated using:

### Learner Feedback
Collect learner feedback on clarity, relevance, difficulty, and usefulness of the training.

### Assessment Results
Review quiz, practical, and final assessment results to identify areas where learners performed well or struggled.

### Practical Performance
Observe whether learners can transfer classroom knowledge into practical tasks.

### Program Improvement
Use assessment results and learner feedback to improve explanations, activities, pacing, and assessment questions in future iterations.

---

## 12. Design Rationale

The program progresses from foundational knowledge to guided practice and independent application.

The design intentionally aligns:

**Learning Objectives → Content → Activities → Assessment → Evaluation**

This alignment helps ensure that learners are taught the knowledge and skills they are later expected to demonstrate.

The program also combines explanations, visual materials, demonstrations, discussion, hands-on practice, feedback, and evaluation to create a structured learning experience for technical learners.
